CLASSROOM MULTIPLAYER SIMULATION · REAL 605 / 310 / 220 · CHAPMAN UNIVERSITY

Use predictive models to underwrite properties, compete for acquisitions, manage the portfolio, and finish with the highest fund value.

~60–75 min gameplay  ·  teams of 2–4 funds  ·  4 scored rounds (+ practice)  ·  winner = highest portfolio NAV  ·  status: gameplay overview for team selection & feedback — Sept 18, 2026

The one loop, in plain language

Students receive data before class and build their model at home. Class time is for running the fund — not for training models.

  1. 1
    Market update — rates, vacancy and rents move; new properties list for sale
  2. 2
    Underwrite — each team applies its own model outputs to the listings
  3. 3
    Allocate capital & bid — sealed-bid auction, within cash and leverage limits
  4. 4
    Acquire — highest valid bid above the hidden reserve wins and pays its own price
  5. 5
    Operate & respond — rent arrives, tenants leave, maintenance and operating surprises hit owned buildings
  6. 6
    Portfolio update & feedback — cash, debt, values and income update; teams see why
  7. 7
    Leaderboard & checkpoint — NAV standings, state saved, next round opens
What the game teaches: a model is only useful because it changes a decision. Every prediction in the game maps to a real choice — what to bid, how much to borrow, which building to fight for, and how to manage what you own.

The model ladder

Teams may bring up to three models. Each one exists because it informs a decision — a model that changes no decision is out of the game.

MODEL 1 · ALL COURSES

Acquisition / Value

What is this property actually worth today?
Informs your maximum bid for each listing.
MODEL 2 · 310

Vacancy / Occupancy

How likely is this building to lose tenants?
Informs acquisition risk appetite and management response after you own it.
MODEL 3 · 310

Rent / Income

How will NOI develop over the hold?
Informs expected return and hold / repair strategy.

Data approach

A real-anchored, synthetic teaching dataset for Orange County commercial property — realistic economics, deliberately not reverse-searchable on Zillow.

  • Calibrated, not literal: property cases preserve relative pricing, submarket differences, rent/value and occupancy relationships and property-type economics.
  • One source of truth: the student packet and the game engine run on the same outcome process — no lookalike dataset.
  • Leakage red-team: no hidden outcome ever appears in student-visible features; train/test separation is enforced and reviewed.
  • Replaceable by design: versioned dataset bundles with pinned seeds, so a future residential or second-market variant is a configuration change, not a rebuild.

Open questions — tell us what you think

  • Management depth: how much operating detail helps learning vs. slowing the round? (Rent calls, repair vs. defer, tenant retention — which decisions belong in round time?)
  • Shock cadence: roughly one meaningful operating surprise per round, across about five rounds — too many, too few? (today's build runs four scored rounds plus a practice round)
  • 605 scope: we have taken 605 as valuation-only, no management, so the first real-world run is the rehearsed game — is that right, or should 605 show a light stance surface from the start?
  • 310 dataset: keep the 310 round on commercial property, or add a residential variant so the same engine can be taught with a simpler asset class?
  • Team pick: which slice do you want — game UX, data & models, engine & infrastructure, or classroom ops?
